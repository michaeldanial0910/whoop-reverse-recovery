M2 - INTERPRETABILITY & FORMULA RECONSTRUCTION: SUMMARY
Generated from results/m2_*.csv (2026-10-08). One person's data; an approximation, not WHOOP's formula.

WHAT WAS DONE
- Data: 411 usable cycles (357 train / 54 held-out post-gap test). New rule since M1: a cycle is
  excluded when the previous "nap" lasted more than 6 h. This catches a main sleep that WHOOP logged
  as a nap; it excludes exactly one training cycle.
- Formula model: ridge regression fitted on logit(recovery/100), using the Set B features. Recovery
  is bounded 0-100. Plain ridge extrapolated to impossible values (-47 on one held-out night) when
  HRV went outside the training range. The logit link saturates the way the real score does.
- Attribution: exact grouped Shapley values in recovery points (src/attribution.py). The inputs form
  11 groups, all 2^11 coalitions are enumerated, and every cycle shares one fixed reference B (the
  mean prediction over 100 fixed training cycles). For every cycle the contributions sum exactly to
  prediction - B. Day-over-day differences sum exactly to the change in prediction. The same method
  is applied to both models.

  Together these two account for about 71-79% of the attribution.

DAY-TO-DAY MOVES (402 back-to-back cycle pairs; the attribution question from plan Appendix B)
  Average share of a move: HRV 40% / 46%, sleep amount vs need 24% / 22%, sleep stages 11% / 6%.
  The largest single driver of a move is HRV in 65% / 67% of moves and sleep amount in 30% / 27%.
  The two models name the same top driver in 76% of moves.
  On moves of 10+ points, HRV's share rises to 47% / 54%.

THE RECONSTRUCTED FORMULA, READABLE (effect at a typical day, recovery ~65)
  HRV +10 ms above your baseline        +10.1 points
  Total sleep +1 h (sleep need fixed)    +4.2
  Sleep need +1 h (sleep fixed)          -2.1
  Prior-day strain +5                    -1.0
  Resting HR +1 bpm above baseline       -0.4
  The logit link makes each effect smaller near 0 and near 100. Individual coefficients
  inside correlated inputs are not interpretable on their own. For example, deep sleep % and
  disturbances per hour carry counter-intuitive signs (-1.8 and +1.7) that most likely absorb
  correlation with sleep length. Read the groups, not single rows.

A RULE RECOVERED EXACTLY
  WHOOP's "sleep need from recent nap" equals minus the previous cycle's nap sleep, to the
  millisecond, in all 412 M1 cycles. This was found while checking the outlier cycle whose sleep
  was 6.4x its sleep need.

INTERACTION: DOES SLEEP MATTER LESS WHEN HRV IS HIGH?
  Slightly. An extra hour of sleep is worth ~3.2-3.3 points on high-HRV nights versus 3.7-4.5 on
  low- and mid-HRV nights, and both models agree. In the formula model this comes purely from the
  logit saturation, because its sleep and HRV effects are additive in logit units. XGBoost shows a
  somewhat larger drop (4.5 -> 3.3) and a larger mean interaction (1.8 vs 1.2 points). But low and
  mid HRV look alike, and each tercile holds only 137 cycles. That is weak evidence for anything
  beyond saturation.

STABILITY (Appendix C-7)
  There are 25 refits: the 5 CV-fold training sets plus 20 random 80% subsamples. HRV's share
  ranges 42-50% (formula) and 46-49% (XGBoost) across refits. Sleep amount ranges 19-24% and
  29-33%. The full-data estimate can sit at the edge of these ranges, because every refit trains
  on less data, and smaller-data models spread credit more thinly over minor groups.
  The two models disagree most on sleep stages (formula 9%, XGBoost 4%) and day of week
  (2.4% vs 0.7%). Treat any share under ~5% as "minor", not as a ranked finding.

SANITY CHECK AGAINST WHOOP'S PUBLIC DESCRIPTION (whoop.com, "How does WHOOP Recovery work 101")
  WHOOP lists HRV, resting HR, sleep performance (sleep you got vs sleep you need) and respiratory
  rate, and says SpO2 and skin temperature are "included as well". It states that respiratory rate
  "often doesn't impact your recovery" unless it changes significantly. It publishes no weights.
  Consistent with the models: HRV and sleep-vs-need dominate, and respiratory rate, SpO2 and skin
  temp are small (each 1-4%).
  Different from what you might expect: resting HR carries only 3-5% once HRV is in the model.
  RHR moves with HRV, so the HRV group likely absorbs much of its effect. That is a
  credit-splitting limit, not evidence that RHR is unused.

LIMITS
- This is attribution of the MODEL, not causation. "HRV accounts for 48%" does not mean raising
  HRV by any means raises recovery by that much.
- One person, 411 cycles. The test set is 54 cycles from one stretch, and it is not blind.
- The reference B is 60.7 points (formula) / 58.9 (XGBoost). Per-cycle contributions are relative
  to B, while day-over-day differences do not depend on it.
- WHOOP's own sleep-need number is used as an input, so part of WHOOP's logic is inherited rather
  than reconstructed.
