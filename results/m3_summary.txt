M3 - ANOMALIES, SCHEDULE IRREGULARITY & DAY ARCHETYPES: SUMMARY
Generated from results/m3_*.csv (2026-10-10). One person's data; associations, not causes.

WHAT WAS DONE
- Test window frozen: the evaluated test set stays the original 54 cycles (to 2026-09-26). The 12
  usable cycles synced since then form a forward set. It is used nowhere in M3 and is kept for
  M4 forward testing. M2's test numbers reproduce exactly on the frozen window.
- Analysis set: 411 usable cycles (357 train + 54 test), as in M2.
- New inputs (features.py):
  - sleep-onset clock time, written as hours after 18:00 (05:00 = 29). No main sleep in the data
    starts between 14:00 and 20:00, so this never wraps.
  - circular SD of onset over the previous 7 calendar days (at least 5 nights needed)
  - shift from the previous night's onset
  - number of logged activities per cycle, every sport type including walks

1. ANOMALY DETECTOR (decisions: robust SD, 10 contiguous blocks)
  residual = actual - the formula model's out-of-fold prediction. Training cycles are predicted
  by a model fitted on the other 9 of 10 time blocks; test cycles by the M2 train-fit model.
  Out-of-fold fit on all 411 cycles: MAE 6.64 / R2 0.82.
  Typical error = robust SD (1.4826 x MAD) = 7.41 points. Flag = |residual| > 2.5 x 7.41 = 18.5 points.

  Flagged: 17 of 411 cycles (4.1%). A normal error distribution would flag 1.2%, so the tails are
  heavy. 16 of the 17 came in WORSE than the model expected and 1 better. The median miss on a
  flagged cycle is 22 points. The flag rate is similar in train (4.2%) and test (3.7%).

  The anomalies are not random. They mark two shapes the formula model cannot represent:
    Hours slept     < 4 h: mean residual -10.7, 32% flagged (n=28)
                    6-7 h: +4.0;  7-8 h: 0.0;  > 9 h: -4.4
    HRV vs baseline +30 to +45%: mean residual -11.9, 30% flagged (n=10)
  (a) Very short sleep is punished harder than the model's smooth sleep effect allows. The 16
      worse-than-expected cycles slept a median of 3.6 h, against 7.5 h for unflagged cycles.
  (b) Sleeping past ~8 h earns less than the model credits, and 6-7 h earns more. This fits WHOOP
      capping sleep credit once you reach your need (sleep performance tops out at 100%).
  (c) HRV spikes far above baseline are credited less than the model's slope implies. This fits a
      ceiling on HRV credit.
  In flagged cycles, sleep amount vs need is the largest contribution 53% of the time, against 27%
  over all cycles. HRV is the largest 41% of the time, against 70% overall.
  These shapes are where the formula's biggest misses come from, and they point to a better
  formula (see "Decision for Michael" below).
  Per-cycle table (dates, values, the groups each prediction relied on, the most unusual input):
  data/processed/m3_anomalies.csv (private).

2. SCHEDULE IRREGULARITY (pre-registered: preregistration_m3.json, commit 1d86070, run after the commit)
  OLS in recovery points on 400 complete cycles. Moving-block bootstrap (7-row blocks, 2000
  resamples); Holm correction across the 3 timing terms.
  TOTAL effect: controls are sleep amount & quality, prior load and day of week; HRV/RHR are not controlled.
                                       coef (pts/unit)   95% CI           Holm p
    7-night onset SD, per +1 h  (PRIMARY)    +0.90       [-1.86, +4.52]    0.56   -> FAIL
    |shift from last night|, per 1 h         -0.99       [-2.36, -0.04]    0.12   (raw p 0.04)
    onset clock time, per 1 h later          -0.56       [-1.55, +0.35]    0.44
  DIRECT effect (+ HRV and RHR): every timing term is within 0.3 points of zero.

  Verdict: the pre-registered hypothesis FAILS. There is no detectable penalty for an irregular
  schedule once sleep amount and quality are held fixed. This is an informative null, not just
  "not significant": the CI's lower end is -1.86 points per hour of SD. A typical swing in
  irregularity (the IQR, 0.8 -> 1.5 h) would therefore cost at most ~1.4 points, so a large
  irregularity effect is ruled out for this person.
  Hint only, not a finding: a big night-to-night shift goes with ~1 point less recovery per hour of
  shift (raw p 0.04, not significant after Holm). In the direct model it drops to 0.0, so whatever
  effect exists runs through HRV/RHR.
  Caveat: irregularity can also lower recovery THROUGH shorter or worse sleep, and that pathway is
  controlled away by design. The question tested is "does timing matter beyond how much and how
  well you slept".

3. DAY ARCHETYPES (decisions: sleep that opened the day, all activities, raw + model-adjusted)
  k-means on z-scored [day strain, number of activities, hours slept]; 430 days. Silhouette by
  k = 2..6: 0.34 / 0.34 / 0.37 / 0.35 / 0.31, so k = 4 is chosen. Those scores mean modest
  structure: real groups, but with soft edges. The clusters are very stable: median adjusted Rand
  index 0.96 (5th percentile 0.86) across 50 refits on 80% subsamples.

                                       days   strain  activities  sleep   next-day recovery   next-day residual
    Rest day (laptop day)               159     6.4      0.2       8.0 h   66.3 [63.5, 69.6]   -1.3 [-3.0, +0.4]
    Short-sleep day                      77     9.1      0.7       4.3 h   64.5 [60.7, 69.5]   -2.6 [-5.4, +0.4]
    Active day                          157    13.7      1.4       8.0 h   59.6 [56.0, 63.1]   -0.4 [-1.8, +1.1]
    Many-activities day                  37    14.5      5.2       5.9 h   51.2 [45.0, 58.3]   -2.6 [-5.2, +0.1]
    (activities = centroid count; residual = actual - predicted for the NEXT cycle; mean residual
     over all days is -1.3)

  Next-day recovery differs across archetypes (Kruskal-Wallis p = 0.001). A many-activities day
  is followed by ~15 points less recovery than a rest day. The model-adjusted residual does NOT
  differ (p = 0.70). The gap is fully carried by what the model already sees on the next cycle:
  after a many-activities day the next night is shorter (6.2 h vs 7.4 h) and HRV runs lower (-5.8%
  vs +2.1% of baseline), along with the prior-strain input. Busy days do not hurt recovery through
  any channel beyond those.
  "Overreach" (strain >= 18): 21 days. They do not form their own cluster; they are split mostly
  across the active and many-activities archetypes. Next-day recovery averages 49.3, and the
  residual is +0.2, so again fully explained by the inputs.

LIMITS
- One person; observational. The archetype and timing results are associations.
- The test set is not blind (see M2). The M3 analyses are descriptive or pre-registered on
  train+test together; nothing in M3 re-evaluated the test.
- The out-of-fold residuals for training cycles come from models that saw later data. That is fine
  for spotting anomalies, but they are not forecasts.
- Activity counts include walks that look auto-detected, so "number of activities" partly measures
  how much the band noticed movement.

DECISION FOR MICHAEL (not done; a model change needs a decision)
  The anomaly analysis suggests a "formula v2": a sleep term capped at need (e.g. min(sleep/need, 1)
  plus a short-sleep hinge below ~4-5 h) and an HRV ceiling (a hinge above ~+30%). The shapes
  were found by looking at residuals that include the test period, so any v2 can only be judged
  on CV plus the untouched forward set. It cannot be judged on the old test.
